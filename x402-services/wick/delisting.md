# Delisting and data retention

There are two different actions here, and we use two different words so they are never confused: **interface delist** (a quality filter) and **catalog purge** (warehouse hygiene). Your data survives both.

> **Listing is a quality surface, not a data lease.** Pay once. Stay on the interface while the market is real. If the pool dies, the chart leaves the rail and the series stays in the warehouse.

## A. Interface delist — quality filter for users

Wick users should not have dead or rugged assets thrust in front of them forever.

- **Applies to:** Listed / Integrated assets.
- **How it works:** the listing remains while the pool is alive. If activity collapses and stays collapsed across **several weeks**, the asset leaves the interface. (That is the public version of the rule.)
- **If a listed token rugs:** the evidence stays on the interface for about a month, then the asset is interface-delisted and becomes **data-retrieval only**.

**Reassurance for teams:** this is not "we took your money and can drop you on a mood." A living pool stays. A dead pool does not occupy the rail. The fee bought interface presence for a *living* market.

## B. Catalog purge / archive — warehouse hygiene

The warehouse should not pretend a corpse is a series.

- **Applies to:** cataloged assets, listed or not.
- **How it works:** data remains callable until the LP is gone — no meaningful activity, the pool is effectively dead — and is then moved to a rugged / no-activity store. Retrieval still exists. It is no longer a live catalog row.
- The archive fuse is longer than the interface delist fuse. You are not dropped from the warehouse at the same speed you might leave the rail.

## C. What holders still get after an interface delist

- **History remains callable** — the series stays retrievable.
- **The rug, if there was one, is not memory-holed.** It just stops being a default rail item.
